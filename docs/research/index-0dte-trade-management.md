# Protect 0DTE winners on the underlying, not the premium

Nobody has published a test of how to manage an open *long* 0DTE index option. No study tests percent-off-peak premium trails, breakeven locks, scale-out ladders, or 5-minute exit triggers on long same-day contracts. Every exit parameter traders quote for this is opinion, a short-premium result, or carried over from studies that traded shares. The evidence that does exist points one way. Exit on structural levels of the *underlying* (VWAP, the volatility band, the session midline), check them on a fixed coarse clock, flatten at a hard time, and add a time component, because a long 0DTE option loses value whenever the underlying stalls. The 2026-11-03 trade (bought 0.99, peaked 1.64 at 13:34, sold 1.08 at 14:00, quoted 0.11 by 15:40) shows both sides of that design. The 30-minute structural trail did its main job: it got out at a small profit before time decay took about 90% of the premium. But it gave back about 86% of the peak open profit, because a 0DTE option can swing from +66% to nearly flat inside one half-hour window. That gap can be closed with a profit-gated faster layer and one scale-out. Both are untested additions, and they should be logged and backtested before anyone treats them as better. The wider context is sobering. In the best 0DTE retail study, single-leg long trades and spread costs account for most retail losses, so any rule that adds round trips needs to clear a high bar. Nothing here recommends trading any instrument. Tickers appear only because the cited studies used them, and every number is a parameter range to test, not advice.

## The only tested exits are structural trails on the underlying

The strongest intraday index momentum evidence is Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon's "Beat the Market" (SFI WP 24-97). They build a noise band around the day's open from the average absolute move up to each minute over the prior 14 days. They act only at HH:00 and HH:30, trail longs at the higher of the upper band or VWAP, use **no profit target**, and close everything at the bell. On 1-minute SPY data from 2007 to 2024, with commission and slippage, it returned **19.6% annualized net, Sharpe 1.33, max drawdown about -25%**, in-sample ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/complex-intraday-time-series-momentum-strategy-applied-to-spy/); [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-sp500-etf-spy/)). The repo's `index-intraday-momentum` skill already follows this design. The half-hour clock is the only anti-shakeout mechanism in this research with an evidence base: by construction it ignores noise inside each half hour.

The next question is which structural exit to use, and the answer seems to be that it matters less than having one. Concretum tested four intraday exits on an ATR-band breakout over 2012 to 2026. All four were profitable. **VWAP retrace scored Sharpe 1.16 with the smallest average intra-trade drawdown (about -64 bps)**, session midline 1.12, Parabolic SAR 1.02, and open retrace 0.92. Rankings rotated by year, and the authors recommend ensembling exits rather than picking a winner ([Concretum Substack](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/how-to-manage-an-intraday-trend-trade)). A follow-up paper claims "VWAP & Ladder" exits reach Sharpe above 3. But it optimized every parameter, states no out-of-sample test, and its abstract does not define the ladder, so treat it as an overfitting warning rather than evidence ([BearBull forum abstract](https://forums.bearbulltraders.com/topic/4002-improvements-to-intraday-momentum-strategies-using-parameter-optimization-and-different-exit-strategies/)).

Stop-loss theory explains why structural trails beat tight ones. Kaminski and Lo show that under a random walk, stop rules *always* lower expected return. They add value only when returns have momentum ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:234-254)). Lo and Remorov find that tight stops underperform because of trading costs, and that the reduction in downside is "not substantial" ([CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27891)). Momentum entries bet on persistence, so the stop should give the trend room to persist. A noise-sensitive premium trail does the opposite. The one sweep of 0DTE exits found makes the same point from the short-premium side: across 450+ configurations and about 30k simulated trades, **every profit-target and stop-loss combination reduced or eliminated edge**. Trailing stops were not tested ([spy-0dte-research](https://spy-0dte-research.pages.dev/research/)). Vilkov finds 0DTE option returns "extremely volatile and skewed" and dominated by the underlying's realized skewness ([Hull Tactical](https://hulltactical.com/2026/07/08/0dte-options-tiny-edge-huge-distribution/)). If a long option's edge sits in the right tail, a fixed full-size target cuts off the payoff it depends on.

## Premium is too noisy to be the thesis stop, but theta makes time a stop

No study compares stops on the underlying with stops on the premium for 0DTE. The mechanics settle the question, though. A 0DTE option's value moves with delta, a gamma that rises steeply into the close, intraday IV, and a spread that is large relative to the premium. A premium drawdown of 25 to 35% can come from an underlying wiggle of well under a dollar. The research notes' back-of-envelope estimate (about 0.4 delta, a $1 premium, gamma of a few tenths) is an inference to recompute from live greeks, not a sourced figure. Practitioner opinion therefore puts the thesis stop on the underlying ([DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/dsijarticledetail/solving-the-puzzle-for-setting-a-stop-loss-for-an-option-trade-34039)). The reference trade shows why: it went to about -30% before reaching +66%.

Broker mechanics push the same way. On Robinhood, an options stop-market order is **sell-to-close only**, cannot be entered from 9:30 to 9:45 ET, becomes a market order when triggered, and "is more likely to result in an execution well below the stop price" in a steep decline ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/stop-market-order-options)). A stop-limit order can trigger and then sit unfilled at a stale price ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/stop-limit-order-options)). Robinhood does not say whether option stops trigger on the last trade or on the bid/ask. Its trailing stop exists for stocks only ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/trailing-stop-order)). An agent that polls quotes and sends its own limit exits controls slippage better than a resting stop. A resting stop is still worth having as a wide, catastrophic backstop in case the agent disconnects.

Theta is the reason a long-option version of "Beat the Market" cannot simply hold to the close. tastylive reports an ATM 0DTE losing about **25 cents per dollar of premium in the first two hours**, against about 0.3 cents for 45 DTE ([tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/theta-0dte-options)). A Black-Scholes illustration from this research (constant 15% IV, spot held flat, a model rather than data) makes the curve concrete. ATM value scales roughly with the square root of hours remaining. It loses about 15% from 12:30 to 13:30 and about 42% from 14:30 to 15:30. A contract one strike out of the money at about 0.41 delta goes from 1.21 at 12:30 to 0.11 at 15:45 with no move at all, which closely matches the reference trade's 0.99 to 0.11. In other words, the reference outcome is about what pure time decay produces when the underlying ends near where it started. A practical consequence follows: from 12:30 to 14:00, a flat-spot ATM option keeps only about sqrt(2/3.5) ≈ 0.76 of its value, so the trade needs about 24% from direction just to break even on time.

Costs compound the problem. Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda find that retail lost about **$350,000 per day on SPX options after daily expiries began** (more than 75% of retail SPX option trades are 0DTE). More than $90M of over $125M in losses came from transaction costs. Single-leg debit trades and trades in high-IV contracts drove the losses, while credit and multi-leg trades did better ([FoFI 2024 PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)). A Cboe-funded rebuttal finds customer trades profitable *when held to expiration*, but it still finds significant **intraday-horizon losses**, which is where scalps sit ([Amaya et al.](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)). The cost of a single spread varies with price. An ATM SPY 0DTE quote one penny wide was 6.7% of a $0.15 ask, against 0.7% for the next-day contract, and the average 0DTE spread was 5.8% ([Schaeffer's](https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/bgs/2023/09/22/0dte-options-are-higher-spreads-imminent)). Robinhood fees are small next to that: no commission on ETF options, $0.04 ORF+OCC per contract, and small sell-side fees ([Robinhood fees](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/trading-fees-on-robinhood)). Every extra exit and re-entry pays the spread again, which argues for few exits and for limit orders near the mid.

On strike choice there is no data, only tradeoffs. Contracts at 0.30 to 0.40 delta buy more delta per premium dollar (the model gives 0.38 per dollar at two strikes out of the money, against 0.30 at the money). But they decay faster in percentage terms, pay a larger spread percentage, and lose delta quickly if the move stalls. The common "0.40 to 0.60 delta for scalps" advice has no supporting data. A reasonable inference is to lean toward higher delta the longer the expected hold and the later in the day the entry.

## A 30-minute clock lets 0DTE gamma round-trip a winner

The reference trade's problem was timing, not the rule. The peak came at 13:34, four minutes after a check, and the next check was 14:00. A share position under "Beat the Market" would give back a few tenths of a percent in that window. A 0DTE option with high gamma and 30 more minutes of theta gave back 0.56 of a 0.65 open gain. The skill already exits between checks if price crosses the stop by more than 0.5 × sigma(t) × open, but that guards the *stop*, not the *profit*. No source tests a fix. The research supports a layered design in which the faster layer switches on only after the trade has earned it, so the tested 30-minute logic stays in control during the noisy early phase.

The candidate scheme, every part of it **untested for long 0DTE**, works in stages. **Stage 0** runs from entry until open profit reaches about 1R, where R is entry premium minus the catastrophic premium stop. In this stage the agent uses only the strategy's structural stop on the underlying plus the wide premium backstop, with no tightening. **Stage 1** begins at about +1R. With two contracts, sell one, and move protection so the remainder cannot become a full loser (entry premium, or the underlying level that corresponds to it). The only test of "half at a profit multiple, trail the rest" found is one swing-horizon walk-forward, where it rescued a system that a fixed target had failed ([MOEasymmetry](https://moeasymmetry.netlify.app/articles/the-sell-rule-that-turned-a-failing-system-into-a-passing-one)). **Stage 2** begins at about +2R. Add a fast exit: a 5-minute *close* (not a touch) through VWAP or the prior swing low, or a giveback limit on the peak bid, whichever fires first. Keep the 30-minute structural trail as the outer stop. Requiring a close rather than a wick is widely advised but untested ([JournalPlus](https://journalplus.co/strategies/ema-bounce-strategy)). One self-reported SPY/QQQ rulebook that exits on EMA5/EMA21 closes reports PF 1.34 over 1,092 trades, which is grade C evidence ([Plan H](https://drm-collab.github.io/master-rulebook/plans/plan-h)). With a 0.99 entry and a 50% backstop (R ≈ 0.50), the 1.64 peak was about +1.3R. Under this scheme Stage 1 would have fired, and the second contract would have exited at or above entry. Whether that beats the current rule *on average* is unknown. Rules like this trade more right-tail capture for less giveback, and the Kaminski–Lo result warns that the tail is where momentum pays.

Two time rules complete the design. The hard one: the reference contract decayed to 0.11, and Robinhood stops new same-day ETF option opens at **15:30 ET** and begins closing at-risk expiring positions then ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/options-trading-hours/)). The skill already uses `min(DEEPTRADE_FLAT_BY, sellout_datetime − 10 minutes)`. The soft one is a no-progress exit. The only evidence is a practitioner "exit at scratch if no move in 30 minutes" rule ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/guide-to-0dte-trading-strategies-real-time-data)) and one long-put 0DTE configuration that used a pure 80-minute time exit (PF 1.34, N=234, with 73% of P/L from three months) ([spy-0dte-research](https://spy-0dte-research.pages.dev/research/)). The theta table argues for a shorter no-progress window after midday than in the morning.

## Re-enter and flip only when the signal fires again

The documented index momentum system handles re-entry without any pattern reading. It re-checks the same band condition at every half hour and enters whenever price is outside the band again. In the boundary-stop variant, a stop-out triggers the opposite trade only when price has crossed the *opposite* band, and the VWAP variant has no automatic flip at all ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=45565)). Pullback-to-EMA, higher-low, and cooldown re-entries are practitioner habits with no published test. SMB's advice amounts to "pre-define re-entry setups and don't overtrade" ([SMB Training](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/how-do-i-reenter-after-a-winning-trade)). A pullback gives a 0DTE re-entry a cheaper price but less time for the trade to work, so comparing premiums is misleading. A flip pays two spreads.

Telling a pullback from a reversal is mostly a problem a momentum system does not need to solve. The Zarattini–Aziz 5-minute QQQ opening-range breakout wins only about **24% of trades for +0.13R each** ([Retail Traders Repository](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/trading-research-paper-can-day-trading-really-be-profitable)), and a replication finds net returns near zero after spread and slippage ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). The edge in such systems comes from a few large trend days. An exit on every counter-move removes exactly those days. If the user wants to "take profit when a counter-move starts," that preference conflicts with how these systems make money, and the user should be told so. The testable definition is a close back through VWAP at a check, which means exit, plus a cross of the opposite band, which allows the opposite side.

The large retail studies argue firmly against trading more often for its own sake. In Brazil, among all retail traders who began day trading mini-index futures from 2013 to 2017, **only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage** ([Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). Taiwan day traders lose in aggregate after fees every year, and the most active earn a gross spread too small to cover costs ([Barber et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)). A day with one trade in five hours means the band strategy did what it was designed to do. It does not show a defect.

## SPY–QQQ divergence is a sizing flag, not a signal

At a daily correlation of about **0.95** ([convextrade](https://convextrade.com/compare/spy-vs-qqq/correlation)), holding both indexes buys almost nothing. A 50/50 split carries about 98.7% of a single position's volatility (√((1+ρ)/2), derived), and correlation rises toward 1 in selloffs. The better choice is to concentrate in the index that better fits the signal, with total risk unchanged. Stock-level studies find correlation lowest at the open and rising through the session ([Allez & Bouchaud](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1009.4785)), which suggests divergence in the first hour is routine and divergence late in the day is more unusual. That is an extrapolation, though, and one GARCH study finds an inverted U ([academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/91730028/Modelling_Intraday_Correlations_using_Multivariate_GARCH)). Futures lead the ETFs in price discovery ([Hasbrouck 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i6p2375-2400.html)), but at a half-hour cadence that lead does not matter.

"SMT divergence" means one index makes a new extreme while its correlated pair fails to confirm. It comes from ICT vocabulary, and even a vendor that sells an SMT indicator says **"there is no public statistic"**: no win rate, no control group, no costed backtest ([El Trader Financiado](https://www.eltraderfinanciado.com/en/blog/smt-divergence)). The reference session fit the bearish pattern: SPY made higher highs into 13:30 while QQQ made lower highs from 11:00, and both rolled over after 14:00. One memorable case is exactly how confirmation bias builds a belief in a pattern. The lowest-risk use is to log a mechanical non-confirmation flag at each check and let it *tighten exits or reduce size*, never trigger a reversal entry, until the journal shows what follows it. Relative strength works the same way. Raw percent-from-open is biased toward QQQ, whose volatility runs about 23% against SPY's 19% ([gale-finance](https://gale-finance.pages.dev/compare/spy-vs-qqq)). The natural normalizer is distance beyond each index's own band: (price − open) / band half-width. A practitioner alternative is "Real Relative Strength," an ATR-normalized excess move over about 12 five-minute bars ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/script/2HiCniE7-Real-Relative-Strength/)). No backtest supports picking the stronger index, and requiring both to break would remove sector-led trend days, which are the trades a momentum system lives on.

Breadth and VIX add little that is proven. TICK extremes in either direction were followed by similar 5-day SPY returns ([TraderFeed](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2007/11/nyse-tick-and-intraday-market-movement.html)). VIX and SPX moved in opposite directions on 78% of days from 1993 to 2009 ([Option Strategist](https://optionstrategist.com/node/7416)), and days when both rose were followed by weak short-term returns ([VIX and More](https://vixandmore.blogspot.com/2012/02/implications-of-positively-correlated.html?m=1)). "New session high with VIX also rising" is a reasonable caution flag. It is untested intraday, and the agent must work without it.

## Time of day and news shift the rules more than any indicator

The best-evidenced time-of-day effect is with-trend momentum into the close. The first half-hour return predicts the last half hour on SPY from 1993 to 2013 (R² about 1.6%) ([Gao et al., JFE 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)). Across 60+ futures from 1974 to 2020, the rest-of-day return predicts the last 30 minutes, and the effect is stronger when dealers are short gamma ([Baltussen et al., JFE 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403)). Cboe counters that 0DTE flow is balanced and puts net market-maker 0DTE hedging at **about 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity** at most ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)). Cboe has a commercial interest, and the gamma mechanism was measured before 0DTE took off. For a Robinhood long ETF 0DTE, the 15:30 cutoff means the position mostly cannot capture the closing window anyway. "Pinning" and "gamma-flip reversals" are vendor claims without verified support ([SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/?p=19793)). Volatility is U-shaped, with the closing hour about three times as volatile as lunchtime ([Andersen et al.](https://sites.northwestern.edu/mtv7889/files/2020/11/tod.pdf)). A volatility-scaled band adjusts for that automatically, which fixed-point targets cannot do.

Scheduled releases have a measurable footprint. After FOMC decisions and minutes, conditional volatility jumps immediately and **stays high for about 30 minutes** ([Jubinski & Tomljanovich](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/fire.12143)). Returns respond only to the surprise component, and more strongly to negative surprises ([EconBiz](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/the-effects-of-federal-funds-target-rate-changes-on-s-p100-stock-returns-volatilities-and-correlations/10012753746)). A 14-day band understates that window's range, and a stop on a fast-moving option can fill far through its price. That supports no new entries just before a release and none for about 30 minutes after it, though no source tests that exact rule. No tested detector for unscheduled news exists. The practical proxy is realized range over the last N minutes far above the same time-of-day average that builds the band, and it is an inference.

The PDT question has changed. **FINRA replaced the pattern-day-trader rule ($25,000 minimum and day-trade count) with an intraday margin-deficit standard, effective 2026-06-04**, and lets firms phase in until 2027-10-20 ([FINRA RN 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)). Robinhood's current implementation was not confirmed. The agent should read day-trade status from the broker rather than assume either regime.

## Parameter ranges for agent rules

The table turns the evidence into candidate settings. Its grades mean: A, a peer-reviewed or working paper with a stated method; B, a documented backtest; C, a vendor or self-reported result; D, opinion. Under the repo's conventions, any value a user can tune becomes a `.env` key with its default in `lib.js`, not a number written into a skill.

| Rule | Candidate value or range | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis check cadence | HH:00/HH:30; trail to max(band, VWAP) for longs, ratchet only | A (shares) | Already in skill |
| Exit type ranking | VWAP or midline retrace ≥ PSAR > open retrace; ensemble acceptable | B | Keep VWAP |
| Catastrophic premium backstop | About 50% of entry premium; never tighter than about 30% | Inference from gamma noise | Test against own MAE data |
| Profit-gated fast layer | Arms at about +1R to +2R; 5-min close through VWAP or swing low | D | Untested; log first |
| Scale-out (2 contracts) | Sell 1 at about +1R; protect remainder at entry or better | C (swing) | Untested for 0DTE |
| Peak giveback limit | Stage 2 only; candidate 25 to 40% of peak open profit | None found | Untested |
| No-progress time stop | 30 min (practitioner); shorter after midday per theta curve | C/D | Untested |
| Hard flat | min(FLAT_BY, sellout − 10 min); Robinhood opens end 15:30 ET | A (broker) | Already in skill |
| Re-entry | Only when band signal re-fires at a check; daily round-trip cap in `.env` | B for mechanism | Cap value is user choice |
| Flip | Only on opposite-band cross at a check; new full stop and size | B | Already implied |
| SPY/QQQ non-confirmation | Log flag; may tighten exits or cut size; never reverse | None | Observe before use |
| Index choice | Larger normalized distance beyond own band; then spread and cap filters | C | Untested |

| Scheduled release | No new entries shortly before; wait about 30 min after | A (volatility) | Rule shape untested |
| Strike | 0.35 to 0.50 delta; favor higher delta for longer or later holds | Model only | No outcome data |

## Conclusion

The post-mortem changes the question from "was the 30-minute trail wrong?" to "what is a 30-minute clock missing for a convex, decaying instrument?" The answer is a mismatch the share-based evidence never faced. The tested trail assumes a position whose value moves linearly with the underlying and does not decay. A long 0DTE contract moves nonlinearly and loses value by the hour. So the fix is not to tighten everything, which the theory says costs edge and the cost data says costs spread. The fix is to add protection that switches on only after open profit exists, and to treat time as a stop. Every such addition is unproven, so the most valuable next step is not a new rule. It is a backtest of the existing band signals on actual intraday 0DTE option quotes with realistic fills, comparing the current exit with the staged variant. A second input is the agent's own journal of post-entry adverse and favorable excursion in premium terms, which is the one number needed to set the backstop and giveback levels and which no published source provides.

One uncomfortable implication remains. The trade category the agent runs, single-leg long debit 0DTE, is the category the best retail study ties to losses, and costs make up most of those losses. Exit rules can reduce giveback, but they cannot create edge. Any change that increases trade count, through faster exits, re-entries, flips, or a second index, has to justify its extra spreads against evidence that most active retail index traders do not overcome them.
